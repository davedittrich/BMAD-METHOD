# Research Methods Guide

## Primary Research Methods

### Interviews

- **Structured**: Predetermined questions, consistent format
- **Semi-structured**: Flexible questioning with core topics
- **Unstructured**: Conversational approach, exploratory
- **Best Practices**:
  - Prepare open-ended questions
  - Record with permission
  - Verify quotes before publication
  - Consider subject's motivations and biases

### Document Analysis

- **Official Records**: Government documents, legal filings, reports
- **Personal Documents**: Letters, emails, diaries, social media
- **Organizational Materials**: Internal memos, policies, communications
- **Verification Steps**:
  - Authenticate document source
  - Check document integrity
  - Cross-reference with other sources
  - Consider document context and purpose

### Observation

- **Direct Observation**: Witnessing events firsthand
- **Participant Observation**: Engaging while observing
- **Systematic Observation**: Structured data collection
- **Considerations**:
  - Observer effect on subjects
  - Personal bias in interpretation
  - Ethical boundaries and consent
  - Documentation methods

## Secondary Research Methods

### Literature Review

- **Academic Sources**: Peer-reviewed journals, books, dissertations
- **News Sources**: Newspapers, magazines, broadcast media
- **Gray Literature**: Reports, white papers, conference proceedings
- **Evaluation Criteria**:
  - Credibility of source
  - Currency of information
  - Relevance to research question
  - Methodology quality

### Database Research

- **Public Records**: Court records, property records, business filings
- **Statistical Databases**: Government statistics, survey data
- **Historical Archives**: Newspaper archives, historical documents
- **Search Strategies**:
  - Use multiple search terms
  - Try different spellings and variations
  - Search by date ranges
  - Cross-reference findings

### Expert Consultation

- **Academic Experts**: Researchers and professors
- **Professional Experts**: Industry practitioners
- **Official Sources**: Government officials, organizational leaders
- **Selection Criteria**:
  - Relevant expertise
  - Independence from conflicts of interest
  - Reputation and track record
  - Willingness to go on record

## Digital Research Methods

### Social Media Research

- **Public Posts**: Tweets, Facebook posts, Instagram content
- **Platform Analytics**: Engagement patterns, trend analysis
- **Network Analysis**: Connection mapping, influence tracking
- **Ethical Considerations**:
  - Public vs. private content boundaries
  - Context collapse and misinterpretation
  - Terms of service compliance
  - Subject consent and privacy

### Web Investigation

- **Website Analysis**: Domain registration, hosting history
- **Cached Content**: Internet Archive, cached pages
- **Metadata Analysis**: Document properties, image data
- **Link Analysis**: Backlinks, citation patterns
- **Tools and Techniques**:
  - Reverse image search
  - Website history tracking
  - Domain registration lookup
  - Social media cross-referencing

### Data Mining

- **Public Data Sets**: Government open data, research repositories
- **Web Scraping**: Automated data collection (where legal)
- **Pattern Recognition**: Statistical analysis, trend identification
- **Legal and Ethical Bounds**:
  - Terms of service compliance
  - Copyright and fair use
  - Privacy protection
  - Data accuracy verification

## Verification and Cross-Referencing

### Multi-Source Verification

- **Triangulation**: Confirm information through multiple independent sources
- **Primary Source Priority**: Favor firsthand accounts over secondhand reports
- **Contemporary Sources**: Prefer sources from the time period in question
- **Independent Verification**: Ensure sources are truly independent

### Fact-Checking Process

1. **Identify Verifiable Claims**: Distinguish facts from opinions
2. **Find Original Sources**: Track claims to their origin
3. **Cross-Reference**: Check against multiple reliable sources
4. **Expert Consultation**: Get professional opinion when needed
5. **Document Process**: Maintain clear record of verification steps

### Source Evaluation Framework

- **Credibility**: Author expertise, publication reputation
- **Accuracy**: Factual correctness, error rate
- **Currency**: Information freshness, relevance
- **Coverage**: Completeness, balanced perspective
- **Authority**: Legitimate expertise, institutional backing

## Research Ethics

### Informed Consent

- **Interview Subjects**: Clear explanation of purpose and use
- **Source Protection**: Confidentiality agreements when needed
- **Attribution**: Proper credit for information and quotes
- **Harm Minimization**: Consider potential negative consequences

### Privacy Considerations

- **Public vs. Private Information**: Respect reasonable privacy expectations
- **Vulnerable Populations**: Extra protection for children, trauma victims
- **Cultural Sensitivity**: Respect cultural norms and values
- **Data Security**: Protect sensitive information from unauthorized access

### Professional Standards

- **Accuracy**: Commitment to factual correctness
- **Independence**: Freedom from conflicts of interest
- **Fairness**: Balanced representation of perspectives
- **Accountability**: Responsibility for errors and corrections

## Documentation and Organization

### Research Documentation

- **Source Records**: Complete citation information
- **Interview Notes**: Detailed notes with timestamps
- **Document Tracking**: Version control, authenticity verification
- **Chain of Custody**: Clear record of information sources

### Data Organization

- **Filing Systems**: Logical organization of materials
- **Backup Procedures**: Multiple copies of important data
- **Version Control**: Track changes and updates
- **Access Control**: Secure sensitive information

### Research Timeline

- **Planning Phase**: Define objectives, methods, timeline
- **Data Collection**: Systematic information gathering
- **Analysis Phase**: Pattern recognition, synthesis
- **Verification Phase**: Fact-checking, source confirmation
- **Documentation Phase**: Final organization and presentation
