# Integrity Assessment Checklist (Carter Framework)

Use this checklist to systematically analyze the integrity of individuals, organizations, or actions using Stephen L. Carter's three-step integrity framework.

## Step 1: Discernment Assessment

_Can the subject distinguish right from wrong?_

### Moral Awareness Indicators

- [ ] **Recognizes ethical dimensions**: Acknowledges when decisions have moral components
- [ ] **Identifies competing values**: Shows awareness of conflicting moral claims
- [ ] **Understands consequences**: Recognizes how actions affect others
- [ ] **Acknowledges obligations**: Aware of duties and responsibilities to others
- [ ] **Considers stakeholder impact**: Thinks about effects on all affected parties

### Red Flags: Discernment Failures

- [ ] **Check for absence**: No evidence of the following discernment failures:
  - Claims moral issues are "just business" or "just politics"
  - Shows willful blindness to ethical dimensions
  - Refuses to acknowledge moral complexity
  - Dismisses concerns about consequences to others
  - Claims ignorance of basic ethical principles

### Documentation

- [ ] **Evidence recorded**: Specific examples of moral awareness or lack thereof
- [ ] **Context noted**: Circumstances affecting moral recognition
- [ ] **Patterns identified**: Consistent behavior patterns noted

## Step 2: Acknowledgment Assessment

_Does the subject act according to their discernment?_

### Consistency Indicators

- [ ] **Values alignment**: Actions match stated values and principles
- [ ] **Promise keeping**: Follows through on commitments made
- [ ] **Personal cost acceptance**: Willing to bear costs for doing right
- [ ] **Truth telling**: Speaks honestly even when inconvenient
- [ ] **Moral courage**: Takes right action despite opposition or pressure

### Red Flags: Acknowledgment Failures

- [ ] **Check for absence**: No evidence of the following acknowledgment failures:
  - Says one thing, does another (hypocrisy)
  - Abandons principles when personally costly
  - Shows convenient moral flexibility
  - Makes promises with no intention to keep them
  - Compromises core values for advantage

### Contextual Considerations

- [ ] **Pressure assessment**: External pressures that might affect behavior
- [ ] **Resource constraints**: Practical limitations on moral action
- [ ] **Competing obligations**: Multiple legitimate moral demands
- [ ] **Information limitations**: Incomplete information affecting decisions

### Documentation

- [ ] **Action record**: Specific actions compared to stated values
- [ ] **Consistency patterns**: Track record of following through
- [ ] **Compromise analysis**: When and why compromises were made

## Step 3: Action Consistency Assessment

_Can the subject openly defend their actions on moral grounds?_

### Transparency Indicators

- [ ] **Public defensibility**: Willing to defend actions publicly on moral grounds
- [ ] **Reasoning transparency**: Open about moral reasoning behind decisions
- [ ] **Responsibility acceptance**: Takes personal responsibility for moral choices
- [ ] **Accountability acceptance**: Willing to be judged by moral standards
- [ ] **Honest communication**: Truthful about motivations and reasoning

### Red Flags: Action Consistency Failures

- [ ] **Check for absence**: No evidence of the following consistency failures:
  - Secrecy about moral reasoning
  - Different behavior in public vs. private
  - Unwillingness to defend actions morally
  - Blame-shifting when confronted
  - Treating ethics as public relations

### Public Record Analysis

- [ ] **Public statements**: What they say publicly about their actions
- [ ] **Private behavior**: How private actions align with public positions
- [ ] **Response to criticism**: How they handle moral challenges
- [ ] **Transparency practices**: Openness about decision-making processes

### Documentation

- [ ] **Public record**: Documented public positions and defenses
- [ ] **Consistency analysis**: Public vs. private behavior comparison
- [ ] **Response patterns**: How they handle moral criticism

## Overall Integrity Assessment

### Integrity Indicators Present

- [ ] **Discernment demonstrated**: Clear evidence of moral awareness
- [ ] **Acknowledgment demonstrated**: Actions consistently follow moral understanding
- [ ] **Action consistency demonstrated**: Transparent moral reasoning and accountability
- [ ] **Pattern consistency**: Integrity maintained across different situations
- [ ] **Growth evidence**: Learning and improvement from moral mistakes

### Corruption Indicators Present

- [ ] **Check for absence**: No evidence of the following corruption patterns:
  - Systematic moral blindness
  - Consistent hypocrisy between words and actions
  - Secretive decision-making processes
  - Blame-shifting and responsibility avoidance
  - Using ethics as manipulation tool
  - Different standards for self vs. others

### Contextual Factors

- [ ] **System constraints**: Institutional pressures affecting behavior
- [ ] **Cultural context**: Cultural norms and expectations
- [ ] **Historical context**: Time period and situational factors
- [ ] **Information availability**: Access to relevant moral information
- [ ] **Power dynamics**: How power relationships affect moral behavior

## Assessment Conclusion

### Integrity Rating

- [ ] **High integrity**: Consistently demonstrates all three steps
- [ ] **Moderate integrity**: Generally demonstrates integrity with some gaps
- [ ] **Low integrity**: Significant failures in one or more steps
- [ ] **Insufficient data**: Unable to assess due to lack of information
- [ ] **Mixed patterns**: Different integrity levels in different areas

### Key Findings

- [ ] **Strengths identified**: Areas where integrity is consistently demonstrated
- [ ] **Weaknesses identified**: Areas where integrity is lacking or inconsistent
- [ ] **Patterns noted**: Consistent behavioral patterns observed
- [ ] **Context factors**: Important situational factors affecting assessment
- [ ] **Future indicators**: What to watch for in ongoing assessment

### Recommendations

- [ ] **Verification needs**: Additional information needed for complete assessment
- [ ] **Monitoring suggestions**: Key areas to watch for future behavior
- [ ] **Context considerations**: Important factors to consider in interpretation
- [ ] **Source reliability**: Assessment of information source quality
- [ ] **Follow-up actions**: Next steps for ongoing integrity analysis

## Documentation Requirements

- [ ] **Evidence compilation**: All supporting evidence organized and referenced
- [ ] **Source attribution**: Clear attribution for all claims and evidence
- [ ] **Methodology notes**: Assessment process and limitations documented
- [ ] **Bias acknowledgment**: Assessor biases and limitations acknowledged
- [ ] **Conclusion support**: Assessment conclusion clearly supported by evidence
