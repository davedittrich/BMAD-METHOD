# guide-argument-construction

## Task Overview

Provide real-time guidance during content drafting to construct logically sound arguments using T. Edward Damer's "Attacking Faulty Reasoning" methodology and the five criteria for good arguments.

## Instructions

1. **Structural Guidance (Criterion 1: Valid Form)**
   - Help writer construct arguments with valid logical structure
   - Guide clear connection between premises and conclusions
   - Suggest ways to make logical flow explicit and traceable
   - Identify and help fix invalid reasoning patterns
   - Recommend proper argument organization and sequencing

2. **Relevance Guidance (Criterion 2: Relevant Premises)**
   - Help ensure all premises genuinely support the conclusion
   - Alert to irrelevant information or red herrings
   - Guide focus on evidence that directly addresses the claim
   - Suggest removal or repositioning of tangential material
   - Help distinguish between relevant and interesting-but-irrelevant points

3. **Acceptability Guidance (Criterion 3: Acceptable Premises)**
   - Help writer use premises that rational people would accept
   - Guide proper sourcing and citation of factual claims
   - Alert to controversial premises that need additional support
   - Suggest ways to establish credibility of foundational claims
   - Recommend addressing potential objections to premises

4. **Sufficiency Guidance (Criterion 4: Sufficient Evidence)**
   - Help ensure adequate quantity and quality of evidence
   - Alert to hasty generalizations or insufficient support
   - Suggest additional evidence types needed to strengthen claims
   - Guide appropriate level of evidence for claim strength
   - Recommend balancing breadth and depth of evidence

5. **Rebuttal Guidance (Criterion 5: Effective Rebuttal)**
   - Help writer anticipate and address counterarguments
   - Guide placement and handling of opposing views
   - Suggest steel-manning rather than straw-manning opponents
   - Recommend acknowledging legitimate objections
   - Help explain why counterarguments don't undermine main claim

6. **Fallacy Prevention**
   - Alert to common fallacies during drafting
   - Provide real-time warnings about logical errors
   - Suggest corrections for identified fallacies
   - Help writer recognize and fix reasoning patterns that violate criteria
   - Guide application of Code of Intellectual Conduct

7. **Constructive Strengthening**
   - Suggest specific improvements to argument structure
   - Recommend evidence additions or modifications
   - Guide more precise language for logical clarity
   - Help writer represent their position in strongest form
   - Provide examples of well-constructed arguments on similar topics

8. **Normal Form Construction**
   - Help writer outline arguments in clear premise-conclusion format
   - Guide numbering and organization of claims
   - Suggest making implicit assumptions explicit
   - Recommend revealing logical structure to readers
   - Help ensure all reasoning steps are visible

**Argument Context:**

- What claim or conclusion are you trying to establish?
- What is the subject matter or domain?
- Who is the intended audience (expert, general public, skeptics)?

**Current Draft Stage:**

- Are you outlining, drafting, or revising?
- Do you have existing arguments to review, or building from scratch?
- Which arguments or sections need construction guidance?

**Specific Needs:**

- Are there particular argument weaknesses you're concerned about?
- Do you need help with evidence selection or counterargument handling?
- Are there known objections you need to address?

## Output Requirements

Provide real-time argument construction guidance including:

- Specific suggestions for strengthening argument structure against all five criteria
- Alerts to logical fallacies or criterion violations with corrections
- Recommended evidence types and sources to support claims
- Guidance on effective counterargument handling
- Suggested phrasings for logical clarity
- Examples of strong arguments relevant to the topic
- Checkpoint assessments for each of Damer's five criteria
- Recommendations for making logical structure explicit to readers
- Constructive suggestions following Code of Intellectual Conduct principles
