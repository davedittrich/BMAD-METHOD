# Carter's Integrity Framework

Based on Stephen L. Carter's "Integrity" - A framework for analyzing integrity in actions and decisions.

## The Three-Step Analysis

### Step 1: Discernment

**The ability to distinguish right from wrong**

- Does the person/organization recognize moral dimensions?
- Can they identify ethical issues and competing values?
- Do they understand the consequences of different choices?
- Are they aware of their moral obligations?

**Corruption indicators:**

- Willful moral blindness
- Refusing to acknowledge ethical dimensions
- Claiming "it's just business" to avoid moral consideration

### Step 2: Acknowledgment

**Acting according to what discernment tells you**

- Does the person act consistently with their stated values?
- Do they follow through on moral commitments?
- Are they willing to accept personal cost for doing right?
- Do they speak truth even when inconvenient?

**Corruption indicators:**

- Hypocrisy - saying one thing, doing another
- Convenient moral flexibility
- Abandoning principles under pressure
- Self-serving interpretation of ethical rules

### Step 3: Action Consistency

**Being willing to say openly that you are acting on your understanding of right and wrong**

- Can they publicly defend their actions on moral grounds?
- Are they transparent about their reasoning?
- Do they take responsibility for moral choices?
- Are they willing to be judged by moral standards?

**Corruption indicators:**

- Secrecy about moral reasoning
- Unwillingness to defend actions publicly
- Blaming others or circumstances
- Acting one way publicly, another privately

## Application Framework

### For Analyzing Public Figures

1. **Discernment Check:** Do they recognize moral issues in their decisions?
2. **Acknowledgment Check:** Do their actions align with stated values?
3. **Action Consistency Check:** Can they openly defend their choices on moral grounds?

### For Analyzing Organizational Behavior

1. **Policy Discernment:** Do policies reflect moral awareness?
2. **Implementation Acknowledgment:** Are policies followed consistently?
3. **Public Action Consistency:** Are decisions transparently defended?

### Red Flags (Corruption Indicators)

- Moral relativism when convenient
- Different standards for self vs. others
- Secrecy around decision-making processes
- Blame-shifting when confronted
- Pattern of promise-breaking
- Treating ethics as PR rather than principle

## Integration with Reporting

Use this framework to:

- Assess the integrity of subjects being covered
- Evaluate organizational decisions and policies
- Identify patterns of corrupt behavior
- Provide objective criteria for integrity analysis
- Support claims about character and trustworthiness

Remember: Integrity analysis should be based on observable actions and verifiable patterns, not assumptions about internal motivations.
