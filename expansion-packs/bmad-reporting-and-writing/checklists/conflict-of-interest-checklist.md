# Conflict of Interest Assessment Checklist

Use this checklist to systematically identify and evaluate potential conflicts of interest that might affect credibility, objectivity, or trustworthiness.

## Financial Conflicts of Interest

### Direct Financial Interests

- [ ] **Investment holdings**: Check for ownership in relevant companies or assets
- [ ] **Employment relationships**: Current or recent employment by interested parties
- [ ] **Contractual obligations**: Consulting, retainer, or service agreements
- [ ] **Stock options/equity**: Equity positions in relevant organizations
- [ ] **Board positions**: Paid or unpaid board memberships

### Indirect Financial Interests

- [ ] **Family financial interests**: Spouse/family member financial connections
- [ ] **Investment fund exposure**: Mutual fund or portfolio exposure to interested parties
- [ ] **Pension/retirement funds**: Retirement account investments in relevant entities
- [ ] **Business partnerships**: Partnership interests in relevant enterprises
- [ ] **Supplier relationships**: Financial relationships through supply chain

### Documentation

- [ ] **Disclosure statements**: Review of public financial disclosures
- [ ] **SEC filings**: Public filing review where applicable
- [ ] **Tax records**: Access to relevant tax information
- [ ] **Business registrations**: Corporate registration and ownership records

## Professional Conflicts of Interest

### Career Dependencies

- [ ] **Future employment**: Potential future employment opportunities
- [ ] **Professional advancement**: Career advancement dependent on interested parties
- [ ] **Industry reputation**: Professional standing affected by position taken
- [ ] **Peer pressure**: Professional community pressures
- [ ] **Certification/licensing**: Professional credentials at risk

### Institutional Affiliations

- [ ] **Institutional funding**: Institution receives funding from interested parties
- [ ] **Research grants**: Grant funding from interested organizations
- [ ] **Sponsored research**: Sponsored research relationships
- [ ] **Institutional partnerships**: Formal partnerships or alliances
- [ ] **Policy advocacy**: Institutional policy positions affecting interests

### Documentation

- [ ] **CV/resume review**: Professional background and affiliations
- [ ] **Grant records**: Research funding sources and amounts
- [ ] **Institutional disclosures**: Organization conflict policies and disclosures
- [ ] **Professional memberships**: Relevant professional organization memberships

## Personal and Social Conflicts

### Personal Relationships

- [ ] **Family connections**: Family relationships with interested parties
- [ ] **Close friendships**: Personal friendships affecting objectivity
- [ ] **Social obligations**: Personal debts or obligations
- [ ] **Romantic relationships**: Personal romantic connections
- [ ] **Mentor/protégé relationships**: Professional mentorship affecting judgment

### Social and Cultural Factors

- [ ] **Political affiliations**: Political party or ideological commitments
- [ ] **Religious obligations**: Religious institutional connections
- [ ] **Cultural loyalties**: Ethnic, cultural, or community loyalties
- [ ] **Social status**: Social standing dependent on position taken
- [ ] **Reputation management**: Personal reputation considerations

### Documentation

- [ ] **Social media review**: Public social media connections and positions
- [ ] **Public records**: Marriage, family, and personal relationship records
- [ ] **Political contributions**: Political donation records
- [ ] **Organization memberships**: Social and cultural organization memberships

## Intellectual Conflicts of Interest

### Academic and Research Conflicts

- [ ] **Research investment**: Significant research investment in particular positions
- [ ] **Publication pressure**: Publication record dependent on particular findings
- [ ] **Academic reputation**: Academic standing tied to particular positions
- [ ] **Peer review relationships**: Editorial or peer review relationships
- [ ] **Citation dependencies**: Citation patterns creating bias

### Ideological Commitments

- [ ] **Theoretical commitments**: Strong theoretical or methodological commitments
- [ ] **Publication history**: Pattern of consistent positions over time
- [ ] **Public statements**: Previous public positions creating consistency pressure
- [ ] **Advocacy relationships**: Advocacy organization connections
- [ ] **Intellectual property**: Patents or IP interests in relevant areas

### Documentation

- [ ] **Publication record**: Review of relevant publications and positions
- [ ] **Patent filings**: Intellectual property records
- [ ] **Public speaking**: Record of public presentations and positions
- [ ] **Media appearances**: Historical media positions and statements

## Temporal Conflicts Assessment

### Past Conflicts

- [ ] **Historical relationships**: Past financial or professional relationships
- [ ] **Previous positions**: Prior employment or contractual relationships
- [ ] **Settled disputes**: Past legal or financial disputes with interested parties
- [ ] **Former loyalties**: Previous commitments that might affect judgment
- [ ] **Cooling-off periods**: Recent departure from potentially conflicting positions

### Future Conflicts

- [ ] **Anticipated relationships**: Expected future relationships or opportunities
- [ ] **Pending opportunities**: Job interviews, contract negotiations, etc.
- [ ] **Succession planning**: Expected inheritance or transfer of interests
- [ ] **Retirement benefits**: Future pension or retirement benefits at risk
- [ ] **Legacy concerns**: Reputation or legacy considerations affecting judgment

### Documentation

- [ ] **Employment history**: Complete employment and contract history
- [ ] **Legal records**: Court records of relevant disputes or relationships
- [ ] **Timeline construction**: Chronological mapping of relevant relationships
- [ ] **Transition periods**: Documentation of relationship changes over time

## Severity Assessment

### High-Severity Conflicts

- [ ] **Direct financial dependence**: Subject financially dependent on interested parties
- [ ] **Substantial financial interest**: Large financial stakes in outcome
- [ ] **Career-critical relationships**: Career survival dependent on relationships
- [ ] **Legal obligations**: Contractual or legal obligations creating conflicts
- [ ] **Family survival interests**: Basic family welfare at stake

### Moderate-Severity Conflicts

- [ ] **Indirect financial interests**: Moderate financial exposure to outcomes
- [ ] **Professional advancement**: Career advancement opportunities at stake
- [ ] **Institutional pressures**: Moderate institutional pressure to maintain positions
- [ ] **Social relationship impacts**: Important personal relationships affected
- [ ] **Reputation considerations**: Professional reputation implications

### Low-Severity Conflicts

- [ ] **Minor financial interests**: Small financial stakes in outcomes
- [ ] **Distant relationships**: Weak or distant connections to interested parties
- [ ] **Theoretical commitments**: Academic or intellectual position consistency
- [ ] **Social expectations**: General social or cultural expectation pressures
- [ ] **Historical loyalties**: Weak historical connection to positions

## Mitigation Assessment

### Disclosure and Transparency

- [ ] **Full disclosure**: Complete and honest disclosure of relevant conflicts
- [ ] **Transparency timing**: Timely disclosure at appropriate moments
- [ ] **Disclosure adequacy**: Sufficiently detailed disclosure for evaluation
- [ ] **Ongoing updates**: Updated disclosure as situations change
- [ ] **Accessible disclosure**: Disclosure in appropriate and accessible formats

### Structural Safeguards

- [ ] **Recusal practices**: Appropriate recusal from conflicted decisions
- [ ] **Independent oversight**: External oversight of potentially conflicted activities
- [ ] **Bias acknowledgment**: Recognition and acknowledgment of potential bias
- [ ] **Counter-balancing measures**: Steps taken to counteract potential bias
- [ ] **Separation measures**: Organizational separation from conflicting interests

### Documentation

- [ ] **Disclosure records**: Documentation of conflict disclosures made
- [ ] **Mitigation measures**: Record of steps taken to address conflicts
- [ ] **Oversight documentation**: Evidence of independent oversight activities
- [ ] **Policy compliance**: Adherence to relevant conflict of interest policies

## Overall Assessment

### Conflict Risk Level

- [ ] **High risk**: Significant conflicts that seriously compromise objectivity
- [ ] **Moderate risk**: Some conflicts that may affect judgment but with mitigation
- [ ] **Low risk**: Minor conflicts that are well-disclosed and mitigated
- [ ] **No significant conflicts**: No material conflicts identified
- [ ] **Insufficient information**: Unable to assess due to lack of disclosure

### Trust Impact Assessment

- [ ] **Trust significantly compromised**: Conflicts seriously undermine credibility
- [ ] **Trust somewhat affected**: Some impact on credibility with proper disclosure
- [ ] **Trust minimally affected**: Well-disclosed conflicts with minimal impact
- [ ] **Trust unaffected**: No material impact on trustworthiness
- [ ] **Trust enhanced**: Transparent handling of conflicts increases credibility

### Recommendations

- [ ] **Additional disclosure needed**: Areas requiring better disclosure
- [ ] **Mitigation measures recommended**: Specific steps to address conflicts
- [ ] **Monitoring suggested**: Ongoing monitoring of conflict situations
- [ ] **Independent verification**: Third-party verification of claims recommended
- [ ] **Context consideration**: Important contextual factors for interpretation

## Documentation Requirements

- [ ] **Evidence compilation**: All conflict evidence organized and referenced
- [ ] **Source verification**: Conflict information verified through multiple sources
- [ ] **Disclosure tracking**: Record of all disclosures made and their adequacy
- [ ] **Assessment rationale**: Clear reasoning for conflict severity assessments
- [ ] **Mitigation evaluation**: Assessment of effectiveness of mitigation measures
