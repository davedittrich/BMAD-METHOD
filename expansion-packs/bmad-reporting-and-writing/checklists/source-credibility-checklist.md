# Source Credibility Assessment Checklist

Use this checklist to evaluate the reliability and trustworthiness of sources for your reporting and content creation.

## Author/Organization Expertise

- [ ] **Relevant qualifications**: Author has education, training, or experience in the topic area
- [ ] **Track record**: History of accurate, reliable work in this field
- [ ] **Recognition**: Peer recognition, awards, or institutional affiliation
- [ ] **Specialization**: Focuses on this topic area rather than being a generalist
- [ ] **Up-to-date knowledge**: Recent activity and current understanding of topic

## Publication Standards

- [ ] **Editorial process**: Clear editing, fact-checking, or peer review process
- [ ] **Publication reputation**: Known for accuracy and journalistic standards
- [ ] **Correction policy**: Clear process for handling errors and corrections
- [ ] **Transparency**: Clear about funding, ownership, and potential conflicts
- [ ] **Contact information**: Real people with real contact information provided

## Bias and Independence Assessment

- [ ] **Financial interests**: No undisclosed financial stake in claims made
- [ ] **Political affiliations**: Political biases acknowledged or appropriately managed
- [ ] **Personal relationships**: No undisclosed personal relationships affecting objectivity
- [ ] **Institutional pressure**: Independence from institutional pressure to reach certain conclusions
- [ ] **Ideological consistency**: Not just confirming pre-existing beliefs

## Evidence and Documentation

- [ ] **Sources cited**: Claims supported with specific, verifiable sources
- [ ] **Primary sources**: Relies on primary rather than secondary sources when possible
- [ ] **Multiple sources**: Important claims supported by multiple independent sources
- [ ] **Source accessibility**: Sources can be accessed and verified by others
- [ ] **Methodology clear**: Research or reporting methods are transparent

## Timeliness and Currency

- [ ] **Publication date**: Recent enough to be relevant for current context
- [ ] **Information currency**: Data and facts are current and up-to-date
- [ ] **Context changes**: Aware of how context may have changed since publication
- [ ] **Update history**: Clear about when information was last updated
- [ ] **Historical awareness**: Understands historical context of claims

## Cross-Verification Indicators

- [ ] **Corroboration**: Key facts confirmed by other reliable sources
- [ ] **Expert consensus**: Claims align with expert consensus where it exists
- [ ] **Contradiction assessment**: Contradictory sources identified and evaluated
- [ ] **Independent verification**: Claims can be verified through independent means
- [ ] **Pattern consistency**: Claims consistent with known patterns and precedents

## Red Flags to Watch For

- [ ] **No red flags present**: Absence of the following warning signs:
  - Anonymous sources without justification
  - Sensational or emotionally manipulative language
  - Claims too good/bad to be true
  - Refusal to provide supporting evidence
  - History of retracting or correcting major errors
  - Funding from obviously biased sources
  - Lack of expertise in claimed area
  - Mixing opinion with fact without distinction
  - Attacking critics rather than addressing criticism
  - Using discredited or outdated sources

## Source Type Specific Checks

### Academic Sources

- [ ] **Peer review**: Published in peer-reviewed journal or publication
- [ ] **Citation index**: Well-cited by other researchers
- [ ] **Institution reputation**: Associated with reputable academic institution
- [ ] **Methodology sound**: Research methodology is appropriate and rigorous

### News Sources

- [ ] **Journalistic standards**: Follows standard journalistic ethics and practices
- [ ] **Source protection**: Appropriately protects confidential sources
- [ ] **Fact verification**: Multiple source verification for major claims
- [ ] **Correction record**: Good track record for issuing corrections when needed

### Government Sources

- [ ] **Official capacity**: Information from official, authorized sources
- [ ] **Documentation**: Properly documented and archived
- [ ] **Political independence**: Information not obviously politically motivated
- [ ] **Transparency**: Subject to freedom of information or transparency requirements

### Expert Interviews

- [ ] **Relevant expertise**: Expert's background directly relevant to topic
- [ ] **Current knowledge**: Expert is current with latest developments
- [ ] **Independence**: No obvious conflicts of interest
- [ ] **Quotation accuracy**: Quotes used in proper context

## Overall Credibility Assessment

- [ ] **High credibility**: Source meets most high-credibility criteria
- [ ] **Moderate credibility**: Source has some limitations but generally reliable
- [ ] **Low credibility**: Source has significant credibility issues
- [ ] **Verification required**: Claims require additional verification before use
- [ ] **Documentation**: Assessment reasoning documented for future reference
